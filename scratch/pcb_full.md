# PCB (Potongan Cukai Bulanan / Monthly Tax Deduction, MTD)

Source: Lembaga Hasil Dalam Negeri Malaysia (LHDNM / Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia), Operational Guideline No. 1 of 2024 (GPHDN 1/2024), "Monthly Tax Deduction under the Income Tax (Deduction from Remuneration) Rules 1994 [P.U.(A) 507/1994]", dated 2 January 2024. The original document is in Malay; the text below is a plain-language English rendering that preserves all figures, rates, Act and rule references, and form names.

Throughout, "the Rules" or "KKCP PDS" refers to the Income Tax (Deduction from Remuneration) Rules 1994 [P.U.(A) 507/1994], "the ITA" or "ACP" refers to the Income Tax Act 1967, and "DGIR" (KPHDN) refers to the Director General of Inland Revenue.

## 1. Introduction

This guideline is intended to help employers determine the Monthly Tax Deduction (PCB) amount correctly and accurately, in line with the Income Tax (Deduction from Remuneration) Rules 1994 and the laws in force on the date of publication.

PCB is a mechanism for deducting income tax from an employee's current monthly remuneration, intended to reduce the burden of paying tax in one lump sum when the actual tax is determined.

Under subsection 107(2) of the ITA, an employer is required to deduct tax from an employee's remuneration each month, or in any month, by the amount directed by the DGIR under rule 3 of the Rules.

From the year 2009 onwards, the PCB amount is determined according to the formula in the Schedule to rule 3 of the Rules, based on either the PCB Schedule (Jadual PCB) or the Computerised Calculation (Pengiraan Berkomputer).

Employers who do not use computerised payroll software are encouraged to use the free e-PCB, e-CP39, and PCB Calculator systems provided on the official LHDNM portal at www.hasil.gov.my via MyTax > ezHasil Services.

## 2. Objective

This guideline explains the employer's responsibilities in implementing PCB on employees' monthly remuneration, and the method of determining the PCB amount.

## 3. Employer Responsibilities

The employer must carry out PCB from each employee's remuneration every month, or in the relevant month, according to the PCB Schedule or the Computerised Calculation, and pay it to the DGIR.

The employer must carry out additional deductions from an employee's remuneration according to a directive issued by the DGIR (Form CP38).

The employer must pay PCB and/or CP38 to the DGIR no later than the 15th day of each calendar month, for the deduction of the previous month.

The employer must submit a complete and accurate employee information statement when paying PCB and/or CP38, containing the employee's name as stated in the identity card or passport, the identity card or passport number, the income tax reference number, and the PCB and/or CP38 amount. If the employer fails to submit complete and accurate data, the PCB and/or CP38 payment will not be processed.

The employer must submit information on the cessation of an employee (Form CP22A/CP22B) through e-SPC, for cases subject to the Tax Clearance Letter (SPC) requirement only.

The employer must safely keep relevant documents relating to its employees for a period of seven years from the end of the year of assessment in which the remuneration was deducted.

The employer must inform each employee of their responsibility to submit Form PCB/TP3 (information on employment with previous employers in the current year, for PCB purposes) for new employees.

The employer must inform each employee of their responsibility to submit Form PCB/TP1 (claim for individual deductions and rebates for PCB purposes) to the employer if the employee wishes to claim deductions and rebates in that month; the employee must safely keep every receipt related to the claim for deductions and rebates for seven years from the end of the year of assessment in which the remuneration was deducted.

The employer must inform each employee of their responsibility to submit complete and accurate personal information and to notify the employer of any change in personal details.

The employer must inform each employee of their responsibility to submit correct information in any form prescribed by the DGIR in relation to tax that may be charged; failure to do so is an offence under paragraph 113(1)(b) of the ITA.

Under paragraph 75A(1)(b) of the ITA, a company director who holds 20% or more of the ordinary share capital of the company is jointly and severally liable for PCB not paid by the company under the Rules.

Action can only be taken against a person who held the position of company director during the period in which the PCB amount became due and payable by the company.

Failure by an employer to comply with subrule 10(1) of the Rules is an offence under rule 17 of the same Rules and, on conviction, is punishable with a fine of not less than RM200 and not more than RM20,000, or imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months, or both.

## 4. Determining the PCB Amount

The PCB amount is determined based on either the PCB Schedule or the Computerised Calculation.

The PCB Schedule applies to employers who do not use the Computerised Calculation. From 1 March 2019, the PCB Schedule is issued in electronic form as the e-Jadual PCB through the e-CP39 system.

The Computerised Calculation applies to employers who use systems or applications developed by LHDNM such as e-PCB, e-CP39, and the PCB Calculator; or employers who use computerised payroll systems provided by software suppliers, or developed or modified by the employer, according to specifications determined and verified by LHDNM.

Under the Computerised Calculation, an employee may claim deductions and rebates allowed under the ITA in any month of the current year using Form PCB/TP1, and the employer is required to process the employee's Form PCB/TP1 not fewer than two times in the current year.

### 4.1 Residence Status of the Employee

The PCB rate differs according to the residence status of the individual, determined under section 7 of the ITA.

For a resident employee, PCB is calculated according to the tax rate stated under paragraph 1, Part 1, Schedule 1 of the ITA, and the employee is entitled to claim the deductions and rebates allowed under the ITA.

For a non-resident employee, PCB is calculated according to the tax rate stated under paragraph 1A, Part 1, Schedule 1 of the ITA.

### 4.2 Employee Category

For the purpose of PCB calculation, employees are classified into three categories. Category 1 is a single employee. Category 2 is a married employee whose spouse is not working. Category 3 is a married employee whose spouse is working, a divorced or widowed employee, or a single employee with an adopted child.

### 4.3 Employment Income

The PCB amount depends on the employment income received by the employee, including remuneration, benefits in kind, and the value of living accommodation provided to the employee.

Remuneration means income in respect of gains or profits from an employment as stated in subsection 13(1) of the ITA. The types of remuneration subject to PCB are salary; wages; overtime payments; commission; tips; allowances; bonus or incentives; director's fees; perquisites; the Employee Share Option Scheme (ESOS); tax borne by the employer; gratuity; compensation for loss of employment; and other remuneration in connection with employment.

A Benefit in Kind (BIK) is a benefit provided by the employer to the employee that cannot be converted into money; it is treated as gross income from employment under paragraph 13(1)(b) of the ITA, and its value is determined according to the relevant Public Ruling on BIK issued by the DGIR.

The Value of Living Accommodation (VOLA) is the value of the living accommodation benefit in Malaysia provided by or on behalf of the employer to the employee; it is treated as gross income from employment under paragraph 13(1)(c) of the ITA, and its value is determined according to the relevant Public Ruling on VOLA issued by the DGIR.

### 4.4 Category of Remuneration

Normal remuneration (saraan biasa) is fixed monthly remuneration paid to an employee, whether the amount paid is fixed or variable, as stated in a contract of service whether written or otherwise. Normal remuneration includes the case where an employee has no monthly salary and only receives commission; where the monthly salary is paid based on daily or hourly rates; or where the monthly salary varies because of changes in the foreign currency exchange rate.

Additional remuneration (saraan tambahan) is any payment additional to normal remuneration in the current month, paid to the employee whether as a lump sum, periodically, in arrears, or as a non-fixed payment. Additional remuneration includes bonus or incentives; arrears of salary or any other arrears paid to the employee; the Employee Share Option Scheme (if the employee elects for PCB deduction); tax borne by the employer; gratuity; compensation for loss of employment; ex-gratia; director's fees not paid monthly; commission not paid monthly; allowances not paid monthly; and other payments additional to the current month's normal remuneration.

## 5. Deductions and Rebates Allowed under the ITA

### 5.1 Mandatory Deductions

The employer must take into account the following mandatory deductions in determining an employee's PCB amount: the individual deduction; the spouse deduction; the child deduction; and the Employees Provident Fund (EPF/KWSP) contribution or other approved scheme.

### 5.2 Additional Deductions

For the Computerised Calculation, the employer must take into account the following additional deductions in determining an employee's PCB amount: the disabled person deduction; the disabled spouse deduction; and the disabled child deduction.

### 5.3 Optional Deductions

For the Computerised Calculation, in addition to the mandatory and additional deductions, an employee may also claim optional deductions allowed under the ITA by submitting Form PCB/TP1.

### 5.4 Rebates

If an employee has paid zakat to the zakat authority through salary deduction, the employer may deduct that zakat payment from the PCB amount that would otherwise be deducted for that month. For example, if the PCB for January 2023 is RM105.00 and zakat paid is RM55.00, the PCB to be deducted after the zakat rebate is RM50.00.

If the zakat paid through salary deduction exceeds the PCB for the month, no PCB is deducted and the excess zakat is carried forward to be deducted from the following month's PCB, provided it is within the same year. For example, if the PCB for May 2023 is RM110.00 and zakat paid is RM140.00, no PCB is deducted and the excess zakat of RM30.00 is carried forward.

If an employee makes any zakat payment other than through monthly salary deduction, the employee may claim that zakat payment by submitting Form PCB/TP1 to the employer in the current year, to determine the PCB amount to be deducted after the zakat rebate.

For the departure levy rebate for umrah travel or travel for other religious purposes, the rebate allowed is equal to the amount of levy paid by the employee for themselves only, and the claim is limited to two trips in a lifetime.

## 6. PCB Calculation Formula

### 6.1 PCB Schedule Formula

The PCB amount based on the PCB Schedule is determined by the formula:

```
PCB = [(P – M) R + B] / 12

where  P = [(Y – K*) × 12] + (∑Yt1 – Kt1*)** + (Yt – Kt*)** – (D + S + QC)
```

P is the total chargeable income for a year.

Y is the current month's gross normal remuneration.

K is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Y, subject to the total amount allowable per year.

∑(Yt1 – Kt1) is the accumulated net additional remuneration paid to the employee in the current year, excluding the current month's additional remuneration; Yt1 is the accumulated gross additional remuneration, and Kt1 is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Yt1, subject to the total amount allowable per year.

(Yt – Kt) is the current month's net additional remuneration; Yt is the current month's gross additional remuneration, and Kt is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Yt, subject to the total amount allowable per year.

The sum of K, Kt, and Kt1 must not exceed the total amount allowable per year, and the (∑Yt1 – Kt1) + (Yt – Kt) terms apply only to the PCB calculation for additional remuneration.

D is the individual deduction, S is the spouse deduction, Q is the deduction for a qualifying child, and C is the number of qualifying children. For Category 1 (single): D = individual deduction, S = 0, and C = 0. For Category 2 (married, spouse not working): D = individual deduction, S = spouse deduction, and C = number of qualifying children. For Category 3 (married with a working spouse, divorced or widowed, or single with an adopted child): D = individual deduction, S = 0, and C = number of qualifying children.

M is the first chargeable income for each band of annual chargeable income, R is the tax rate percentage, and B is the tax on M after deducting the individual and spouse tax rebates, if eligible. Once P is obtained, the values of M, R, and B are determined from Table 1, where the value of B depends on the employee category.

### 6.1.1 Table 1 (values of P, M, R, and B), expressed as sentences

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM5,001 to RM20,000, the first chargeable income (M) is RM5,000, the tax rate (R) on the amount above M is 1%, and B is –RM400 for Category 1 and Category 3 employees and –RM800 for Category 2 employees.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM20,001 to RM35,000, M is RM20,000, R is 3%, and B is –RM250 for Category 1 and Category 3 employees and –RM650 for Category 2 employees.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM35,001 to RM50,000, M is RM35,000, R is 6%, and B is RM600 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM50,001 to RM70,000, M is RM50,000, R is 11%, and B is RM1,500 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM70,001 to RM100,000, M is RM70,000, R is 19%, and B is RM3,700 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM100,001 to RM400,000, M is RM100,000, R is 25%, and B is RM9,400 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM400,001 to RM600,000, M is RM400,000, R is 26%, and B is RM84,400 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) from RM600,001 to RM2,000,000, M is RM600,000, R is 28%, and B is RM136,400 for all categories.

For chargeable annual income (P) exceeding RM2,000,000, M is RM2,000,000, R is 30%, and B is RM528,400 for all categories.

### 6.1.2 Rounding and Minimum (PCB Schedule)

The PCB amount stated in the PCB Schedule is rounded up or down to the nearest multiple of five sen.

The minimum PCB amount under the PCB Schedule is RM1.

If the PCB amount (before the zakat deduction) is less than RM1, no PCB needs to be deducted from the employee's remuneration.

An employer that still uses the PCB Schedule must instead use the Computerised Calculation or the PCB Calculator on the official LHDNM portal if the employee elects to claim deductions other than for self, spouse, child, and EPF; receives a salary adjustment (an increase or decrease); newly starts employment in a month other than January; or newly starts employment with a new employer and has previously received remuneration from a former employer.

### 6.2 Computerised Calculation Formula

The PCB amount based on the Computerised Calculation is determined by the formula:

```
PCB for the current month = { [(P – M) R + B] – (Z + X) } / (n + 1)

Net PCB = PCB for the current month – current month's zakat

where  P = [∑(Y – K*) + (Y1 – K1*) + [(Y2 – K2*) n] + (Yt – Kt*)**]
           – [D + S + DU + SU + QC + (∑LP + LP1)]
```

P is the total chargeable income for a year.

∑(Y – K) is the accumulated net normal remuneration and net additional remuneration for the current year paid to the employee up to the month before the current month, including net normal and net additional remuneration paid by a former employer, if any; Y is the corresponding accumulated gross figure; and K is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Y in the current year, including that claimed under previous employment, subject to the total amount allowable per year.

Y1 is the current month's gross normal remuneration, and K1 is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Y1 in the current month, subject to the total amount allowable per year.

Y2 is the estimated remuneration (like Y1) for the following month, and K2 is the estimated balance of EPF or other approved scheme contribution for the remaining eligible months, calculated as [total amount allowable per year – (K + K1 + Kt)] / n, or K1, whichever is lower.

(Yt – Kt) is the current month's net additional remuneration; Yt is the current month's gross additional remuneration, and Kt is the EPF or other approved scheme contribution paid in respect of Yt, subject to the total amount allowable per year. The sum of K, K1, K2, and Kt must not exceed the total amount allowable per year, and the ∑(Yt – Kt) term applies only to the PCB calculation for additional remuneration.

n is the number of remaining months in the year, and (n + 1) is the number of remaining months in the year including the current month.

D is the individual deduction, S is the spouse deduction, DU is the disabled person deduction, SU is the disabled spouse deduction, Q is the deduction for a qualifying child, and C is the number of qualifying children. The values of D, S, and C are set by category exactly as in the PCB Schedule formula: for Category 1, D = individual deduction, S = 0, C = 0; for Category 2, D = individual deduction, S = spouse deduction, C = number of qualifying children; for Category 3, D = individual deduction, S = 0, C = number of qualifying children.

∑LP is the accumulated allowable deductions in the current year, including from previous employment if any, and LP1 is the allowable deduction for the current month.

M is the first chargeable income for each band of annual chargeable income, R is the tax rate percentage, and B is the tax on M after deducting the individual and spouse tax rebates, if eligible (values of M, R, and B are taken from Table 1).

Z is the accumulated zakat paid in the current year other than the current month's zakat, and X is the accumulated PCB paid for previous months in the current year, including payments from previous employment, but excluding additional PCB requested by the employee and tax instalment payments.

### 6.2.1 Computerised Calculation for the Returning Expert Programme (PKP)

The Returning Expert Programme (PKP) is a programme managed by Talent Corporation Malaysia Berhad and approved by the Government, meeting the conditions in P.U.(A) 151/2012.

An approved employee may elect to be charged a flat tax rate of 15% on chargeable income from employment as provided under Part XV, Schedule 1 of the ITA.

The election is available for a period of five consecutive years of assessment, beginning from the first year of assessment chosen by the employee.

If the total chargeable income does not exceed RM35,000.00, the employee is entitled to an individual, or spouse, tax rebate of RM400.00 each.

The PCB for an eligible employee is determined by the formula:

```
PCB for the current month = [(PR – T) – (Z + X)] / (n + 1)

Net PCB = PCB for the current month – current month's zakat
```

P is defined as in the general Computerised Calculation. R is the tax rate percentage (15%), and T is the individual or spouse rebate, if any. Under Table 2, for total chargeable income (P) of RM35,000 and below, R is 15% and T is RM400 for Category 1 and Category 3 employees and RM800 for Category 2 employees; for P exceeding RM35,000, R is 15% and T is RM0 for all categories.

### 6.2.2 Computerised Calculation for Knowledge Workers in a Specified Region

A knowledge worker is an eligible person who holds a bachelor's or master's degree in any professional or technical field from a college, institution, or university recognised by the Government of Malaysia and has at least ten years of working experience in any qualifying activity; or who holds a doctorate in any professional or technical field from a recognised college, institution, or university and has at least five years of working experience in any qualifying activity, and who has met any other criteria as determined by the Minister as prescribed in P.U.(A) 344/2010 and 330/2023.

An approved employee may elect to be charged a flat tax rate of 15% on chargeable income from employment as provided under Part XIV, Schedule 1 of the ITA, for as long as they continue to reside and work in the relevant region.

If the total chargeable income does not exceed RM35,000.00, the employee is entitled to an individual, or spouse, tax rebate of RM400.00 each.

The PCB is determined by the same formula as for the Returning Expert Programme, and under Table 3 the values are the same as Table 2: for total chargeable income (P) of RM35,000 and below, R is 15% and T is RM400 for Category 1 and Category 3 employees and RM800 for Category 2 employees; for P exceeding RM35,000, R is 15% and T is RM0.

### 6.2.3 Computerised Calculation for a Non-Citizen Resident Holding a C-Suite Position in an Approved Company

The eligible employee is a non-citizen resident individual employed by an approved company and holding a C-Suite position throughout the specified year of assessment, meeting the conditions prescribed in P.U.(A) 242/2023.

A C-Suite position means a senior top executive position dependent on functional expertise and technical skills, such as Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Operating Officer, and Chief Information Officer.

The PCB for an eligible employee is determined by the formula:

```
PCB for the current month = [PR – (Z + X)] / (n + 1)

Net PCB = PCB for the current month – current month's zakat
```

P is defined as in the general Computerised Calculation, R is the tax rate percentage, Z is the accumulated zakat paid in the current year other than the current month's zakat, and X is the accumulated PCB paid for previous months in the current year (including from previous employment, but excluding additional PCB requested by the employee and tax instalment payments).

### 6.2.4 Rounding and Minimum (Computerised Calculation)

Under the Computerised Calculation, the computation is limited to two decimal places, ignoring subsequent digits.

The PCB value is rounded to the nearest five sen as follows: a third-decimal digit of one, two, three, or four is rounded to five sen; and a third-decimal digit of six, seven, eight, or nine is rounded to ten sen.

If the PCB amount before the zakat deduction is less than RM10, the employer is not required to make PCB.

If the PCB amount after the zakat deduction is less than RM10, the employer is required to make PCB.

The minimum PCB amount under the Computerised Calculation is RM10.

## 7. Determining the PCB Amount for Additional Remuneration

If an employee receives additional remuneration other than normal remuneration, the tax to be deducted and paid in that month is calculated using the Additional Remuneration Formula.

### 7.1 Additional Remuneration Based on the PCB Schedule

Step 1 — Determine the PCB on net normal remuneration for the year: determine the employee category; determine net normal remuneration as gross normal remuneration minus the EPF or other approved scheme contribution (subject to the total amount allowable per year); based on that net normal remuneration, refer to the PCB Schedule to determine the current month's PCB; determine the current month's net PCB as the current month's PCB minus the current month's zakat, if any; and determine the total PCB for the year as X + [current month's PCB × (n + 1)], where X is the accumulated PCB already paid and (n + 1) is the remaining months in the year including the current month.

Step 2 — Determine the chargeable income for the year, including the current month's additional remuneration, using the formula P = [(Y – K) × 12] + [∑(Yt1 – Kt1)] + (Yt – Kt) – (D + S + QC).

Step 3 — Determine the total tax for the year as (P – M) R + B, where P is from Step 2 and M, R, and B are from Table 1.

Step 4 — Determine the PCB for the current month's additional remuneration as: total tax for the year (Step 3) – total PCB for the year (Step 1) + zakat already paid.

Step 5 — Determine the current month's PCB payable as: net PCB (Step 1) + PCB for the current month's additional remuneration (Step 4).

Worked example (Example 3): A married Category 3 employee (KA2) with a working spouse and two qualifying children, for January 2023, has normal remuneration of RM4,600.00 with EPF of RM506.00 and additional remuneration of RM9,200.00 with EPF of RM1,012.00. Net normal remuneration is RM4,600.00 – RM333.00 = RM4,267.00 (the monthly EPF contribution is limited to RM333.00, reflecting the RM4,000 annual cap on the allowable EPF deduction). The PCB Schedule gives a current month's PCB of RM71.35, so net PCB is RM71.35 and the total PCB for the year is RM0.00 + (RM71.35 × 12) = RM856.20. Chargeable income for the year is [(RM4,600.00 – RM333.00) × 12] + [RM9,200.00] – [RM9,000.00 + RM0.00 + RM4,000.00] = RM47,404.00, using the individual deduction of RM9,000.00, spouse deduction of RM0.00 (Category 3), and child deduction of RM4,000.00 (RM2,000.00 per child × two children). Total tax for the year is (RM47,404.00 – RM35,000.00) × 8% + RM600.00 = RM1,592.32. The PCB on the additional remuneration is RM1,592.32 – RM856.20 = RM736.12, so the current month's PCB payable is RM736.12.

### 7.2 Additional Remuneration Based on the Computerised Calculation

Step 1 — Determine the PCB on net remuneration for the year, excluding the current month's additional remuneration: determine the employee category; determine chargeable income for the year (excluding the current month's additional remuneration) using the paragraph 6.2 formula; determine the current month's PCB on net normal remuneration using the paragraph 6.2 formula PCB = {[(P – M) R + B] – (Z + X)} / (n + 1) with M, R, and B from Table 1; determine net PCB as that current month's PCB minus the current month's zakat; and determine the total PCB for the year as X + [current month's PCB × (n + 1)].

Step 2 — Determine the chargeable income for the year, including the current month's additional remuneration, using the paragraph 6.2 formula.

Step 3 — Determine the total tax for the year as (P – M) R + B, with P from Step 2 and M, R, and B from Table 1.

Step 4 — Determine the PCB for the current month's additional remuneration as: total tax for the year (Step 3) – total PCB for the year (Step 1) + zakat already paid.

Step 5 — Determine the current month's PCB payable as: net PCB (Step 1) + PCB for the current month's additional remuneration (Step 4).

Worked example (Example 4): For the same married Category 3 employee (KA2) with a working spouse and two qualifying children, for January 2023, with normal remuneration of RM4,600.00 (EPF RM506.00) and additional remuneration of RM9,200.00 (EPF RM1,012.00), the Computerised Calculation gives a Step 1 net PCB of RM71.33 and a total PCB for the year of RM855.96, a Step 2 chargeable income of RM47,399.96, a Step 3 total tax for the year of (RM47,399.96 – RM35,000.00) × 8% + RM600.00 = RM1,592.00, a Step 4 PCB on additional remuneration of RM1,592.00 – RM855.96 = RM736.04, and a Step 5 current month's PCB payable of RM71.33 + RM736.04 = RM807.37.

The PCB for the current month's normal remuneration and the current month's additional remuneration must be made in a single payment.
